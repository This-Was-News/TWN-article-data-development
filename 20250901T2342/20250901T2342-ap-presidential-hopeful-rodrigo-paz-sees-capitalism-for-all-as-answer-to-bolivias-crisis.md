## Article summary
Rodrigo Paz, a centrist Bolivian presidential front-runner, is campaigning on a platform of 'capitalism for all' to address Bolivia's severe economic crisis. In an interview with the Associated Press, Paz emphasized a pragmatic and diverse approach to governance, appealing to voters across the political spectrum. His campaign, alongside running mate Edman Lara, combines economic deregulation and cost-cutting with social programs like pension increases and universal income for stay-at-home wives and mothers. Paz promises to end costly fuel subsidies while maintaining assistance for children and the elderly, arguing that eliminating state corruption and waste will restore fiscal order. He refuses to specify the affordability of his social spending measures, citing the need to assess the government's financial situation after taking office. Paz rejects accusations of populism, asserting that his policies are national, democratic, and popular. He aims to attract voters from El Alto, a city known for its entrepreneurial spirit and skepticism towards international monetary interventions. Paz plans to keep strategic state-owned companies in public hands while privatizing only loss-making ones, restricting sales to Bolivian buyers. He rules out an IMF rescue package but is open to foreign support on Bolivia's terms. Analysts note that Paz's political capital lies in his ambiguity, which has helped him gain support in the lead-up to the hotly contested presidential runoff against former right-wing President Jorge Tuto Quiroga.
