## Article summary
Tens of thousands of anti-government protesters marched in Serbia on Monday, demanding justice and early elections, as they protested against President Aleksandar Vučić's populist rule. The protests, led by students, marked 10 months since a fatal canopy collapse in Nov Sad, which sparked public outrage over state corruption and negligence. Protests occurred in Belgrade and other towns, with police using force in Nov Sad. Vučić's headquarters were heavily guarded by police and paramilitary loyalists. Protesters also demand transparent investigations into the collapse, criminal prosecutions, and free media, which Vučić is trying to suppress. A student protester expressed determination to continue fighting for change. Over a hundred professors have been dismissed for supporting the students and replaced by Vučić's loyalists. Meanwhile, Vučić attended a gathering in China with other world leaders, including Russia's Vladimir Putin. Vučić has labeled the protesters as terrorists aiming to remove him from power. Thousands of his supporters also marched in several towns, highlighting deep divisions in the country.
