## Article summary
Over Labor Day weekend, the Trump administration attempted to remove Guatemalan children living in U.S. shelters or foster care, but a federal judge temporarily halted the removals. Advocates argue the children fear returning home and that the government is bypassing legal protections. Three legal cases are ongoing in Arizona, Washington D.C., and Illinois, with judges ordering temporary halts to the removals. Guatemalan President Bernardo Arévalo stated his government is willing to receive unaccompaned minors who wish to return voluntarily. The exact number of children to be sent back is unclear, with estimates ranging from 341 to nearly 700. The U.S. government indicated 76 children were on planes to Guatemala but were returned after a judge's ruling. Migrant children have legal protections under the Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act of 2008, which allows them to apply for special status or asylum. Advocates emphasize that only an immigration judge can decide if a child can voluntarily depart the U.S.
