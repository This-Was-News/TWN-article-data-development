## Article summary
Donald Trump's shift in presidential admiration from Andrew Jackson to William McKinley reflects his evolving political goals. Initially favoring Jackson's populist approach, Trump now aligns with McKinley's policies, particularly tariffs, which are a key focus of his second term. This shift is evident in Trump's actions, such as renaming North America's tallest peak to Mount McKinley and implementing significant tariffs. Trump's current advisors, many from the financial sector, support his economic policies, which target foreign countries and globalists embracing free trade. Trump aims to impose a new economic order with steep import taxes to negotiate better trade deals. Despite Jackson's past influence, Trump's current policies and rhetoric align more with McKinley's expansionist and protectionist agenda. Historically, McKinley's tariffs led to economic stratification, but Trump sees them as a means to protect American interests. This shift in presidential heroes underscores Trump's transactional approach to history and policy, using McKinley as a justification for his tariff-centric economic strategy.
