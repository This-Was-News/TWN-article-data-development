## Article summary
President Trump, now seven months into his second term, claims to be fulfilling his campaign promises to American workers, asserting that his administration's policies aim to boost blue-collar jobs and rebuild the nation's industrial base. However, labor leaders strongly disagree, with AFL-CIO President Liz Shuler stating that this administration has been the most hostile to workers in their lifetimes. While economic indicators like wage growth and low unemployment suggest a strong job market, there is significant uncertainty and tension among workers. Trump's immigration policies, including the end of programs that provided relief to hundreds of thousands of people, are disrupting industries like agriculture and healthcare, where American workers are not readily filling the vacancies left by departing immigrants. Union leaders, including those representing Trump's base, are concerned about the future of infrastructure projects initiated under the previous administration and the impact of the Trump administration's stop-work order on the Revolution Wind project, which has left several hundred workers unemployed. Additionally, the federal workforce is undergoing a significant upheaval, with around 300,000 federal employees expected to leave by year's end, raising concerns about the government's efficiency and leadership stability. Other notable topics include the campaign to reinstate paid sick leave in Missouri, the decline in tourism at Utah's national parks, the 50th-anniversary revival of the film Jaws, and a discussion on the effectiveness of U.S.-made sunscreens compared to international alternatives.
