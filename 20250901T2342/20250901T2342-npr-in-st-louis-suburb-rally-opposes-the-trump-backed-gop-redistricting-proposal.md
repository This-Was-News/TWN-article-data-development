## Article summary
A rally in Clayton, Missouri, brought together hundreds of people protesting Governor Mike Kehoe's decision to call a special legislative session to redraw congressional districts and alter the state's initiative petition process. Don Looney, a UAW member, urged the crowd to go beyond expressing anger and actively fight for their rights. Kehoe's proposal aims to convert Democratic Congressman Emanuel Cleaver's district into a Republican-leaning seat and requires constitutional amendments for state-wide and congressional district initiatives. Critics argue this move is politically motivated to secure Republican dominance. State Sen. Doug Beck highlighted the disconnect between Missouri's progressive ballot initiatives and conservative lawmakers. Rallygoers, like Webster Groves resident Leslie Hnyard, see this as a turning point against GOP control, citing fear of the 2026 midterm elections. Others, such as St. Louis County resident Aranna Kmbrough, view it as Republican overreach, following previous controversies like gutting a voter-approved paid sick leave initiative. The proposed map could backfire if the political environment shifts favorably for Democrats. Democrats plan to challenge the map in court and may push for a commission to handle redistricting in the future. Overall, the special session is seen as a contentious move that could confuse voters and further polarize the state's political landscape.
