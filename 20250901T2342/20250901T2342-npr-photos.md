## Article summary
The article explores how humans are manipulating and altering the natural world, focusing on the concept of the Anthropocene, a term describing the significant impact humans have had on the planet. Photographer Zed Nelson's work highlights the illusion of untouched nature, showcasing how tourism, infrastructure, and commercialization have created curated versions of the wild. Nelson's book, 'The Anthropocene Illusion,' captures the paradoxical relationship between humans and nature, revealing the managed and often artificial environments presented to tourists. Examples include Kenya's safari industry, where tourists experience staged scenes like champagne breakfasts and reenactments from movies, while the reality of declining wildlife populations is ignored. The article also discusses the pressure on national parks, such as Nairobi National Park, which faces urban expansion and infrastructure projects like the Chinese-built railway. Nelson's photographs depict the stark contrast between the illusion of natural habitats and the harsh reality of captivity and exploitation, such as the largest captive herd of elephants in Sri Lanka and the confined polar bear in a Chinese zoo. The article concludes by emphasizing the need to reassess what we value and the lasting impact humans will leave on the planet.
