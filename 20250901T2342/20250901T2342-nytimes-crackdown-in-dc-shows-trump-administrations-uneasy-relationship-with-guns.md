## Article summary
The Trump administration's crackdown on crime in Washington, D.C., has involved aggressive gun seizures, with around 150 weapons confiscated since the president declared a crime emergency. Attorney General Pam Bond and U.S. Attorney Jeanne Prro have led efforts, resulting in numerous arrests and gun seizures. This shift marks a departure for an administration that has otherwise rolled back gun regulations and reduced federal firearms enforcement. The administration has cut funding and personnel for the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), causing unease among gun-rights supporters. Despite these cuts, federal firearms prosecutions in D.C. have increased. The administration's policies have been criticized as politically motivated rather than effective crime-fighting strategies. Local officials have cautiously supported the increased federal enforcement, but the long-term impact on crime remains uncertain. The crackdown has led to collaborations between local and federal law enforcement, resulting in numerous arrests and prosecutions. Jeanne Prro, a Trump loyalist, has supported aggressive gun enforcement but has also made policy shifts to align with the administration's stance on gun rights. The overall effect of these measures on crime in D.C. is still under scrutiny.
