## Article summary
The article discusses the dangers of taking selfies, particularly in India, where a significant number of selfie-related deaths have been reported. In 2017, a study found that over half of the 127 selfie deaths worldwide occurred in India. In response, Indian authorities identified high-risk zones and launched awareness campaigns, including a safety app called Safte. Despite these efforts, India remains at the top of the global list for dangerous selfies, with recent surveys showing a high number of incidents. The most common danger zones include heights, waterfalls, and cliffs, with men being the primary instigators. The pursuit of online validation and the desire for internet fame often lead to risky behavior. Hashtags like #dangerousselfe encourage copycat actions, and authorities are now concerned about selfies taken at massive gatherings, which can pose additional risks. Police have implemented measures such as temporary phone confiscation to prevent accidents. The article highlights the ongoing problem of risky selfies and the efforts to mitigate their dangers.
