## Article summary
The article discusses the ongoing issue of risky selfies, particularly in India, where they are a leading cause of injury and death. In 2017, a study found that over half of the 127 selfie-related deaths worldwide occurred in India. In response, Indian authorities launched awareness campaigns and developed apps to warn people about dangerous selfie spots. However, recent incidents, such as elephants charging at tourists attempting selfies, show that the problem persists. Surveys in 2025 reveal that India still leads in dangerous selfies, with men being the primary instigators. Common danger zones include heights, waterfalls, and rooftops. The pursuit of online validation and social media fame drives this behavior, with hashtags like #dangerousselfe encouraging risky actions. Additionally, mass gatherings pose new risks, as selfie-takers can disrupt crowd control and cause accidents. Authorities are taking measures, such as banning selfies at large events, to prevent tragedies.
