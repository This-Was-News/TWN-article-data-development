## Article summary
The Guatemalan government stated on August 31 that it proposed to U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem that unaccompanied Guatemalan minors be returned to their home country. The government aims to reunite these children with their families and support them through social programs, asserting that all actions comply with human rights and due process. However, the Guatemalan government did not immediately respond to questions about the number of children requested for return or parental consent. The statement contained a factual error regarding the timing of Noem's visit, which occurred on June 26, not July. During her visit, Noem and Guatemalan Interior Minister Francisco Jimenez signed agreements, including one potentially allowing non-Guatemalan asylum seekers to seek asylum in Guatemala instead of the U.S. Attorneys representing the children dispute the government's claim, arguing that the deportation process violates federal laws and the U.S. Constitution. The National Immigration Law Center successfully obtained a temporary restraining order to halt the deportations, vowing to continue defending the children's right to stay in the U.S.
