## Article summary
The web page discusses photographer Zed Nelson's exploration of the concept of the Anthropocene, a term describing the significant impact humans have had on the planet. Nelson's work, captured in his book 'The Anthropocene Illusion,' focuses on how human activities are altering our relationship with nature. Over six years, he traveled to 14 countries, visiting national parks, theme parks, zoos, and hotels to document the contrived and paradoxical ways we interact with the natural world. His photographs reveal the illusion of managed, curated versions of nature, such as staged safaris and captive elephant herds. Nelson highlights the irony of these creations, which often serve as monuments to the very aspects of nature we are losing. He spent two days observing a polar bear in a Chinese zoo, noting the stark contrast between its tiny enclosure and the vast Arctic wilderness it should inhabit. The page also mentions Nelson's visit to Kenya, where tourists can recreate scenes from movies, and his observations of animals in zoos that appear listless or mournful. Nelson's work aims to spark a conversation about what we value and how we can reassess our relationship with the natural world.
