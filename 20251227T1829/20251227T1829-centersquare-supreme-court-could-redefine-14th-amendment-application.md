## Article summary
The U.S. Supreme Court is set to hear the case Trump v. Barbara in 2026, which challenges President Donald Trump's executive order aiming to end birthright citizenship for children born to parents who are in the U.S. illegally or are temporary residents. This case hinges on the interpretation of the phrase "subject to the jurisdiction thereof" within the 14th Amendment, which currently is understood to grant citizenship to all individuals born in the U.S. Legal experts Michael Ramsey and Ilan Wurman offer differing perspectives on this clause. Ramsey argues that the 14th Amendment applies to all children born in the U.S., regardless of their parents' legal status, as they are subject to U.S. laws. Wurman, however, contends that protection under the sovereign's law, derived from English common law, is a prerequisite for citizenship, suggesting that those who enter unlawfully may not be considered under this protection. The court's decision could significantly redefine the application of the 14th Amendment regarding birthright citizenship. Oral arguments are anticipated in early 2026, though a specific date has not yet been scheduled.
