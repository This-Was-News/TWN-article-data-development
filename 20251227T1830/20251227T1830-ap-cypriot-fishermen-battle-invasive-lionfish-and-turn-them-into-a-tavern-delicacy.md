## Article summary
Cypriot fishermen are facing a significant challenge due to the proliferation of invasive lionfish in the Mediterranean Sea. These fish, originating from the Red Sea, threaten native fish populations and disrupt the livelihoods of local fishermen. Warmer sea temperatures, exacerbated by climate change, are identified as the primary driver for the increased presence of these invasive species. The silver-cheeked toadfish, another invasive species, also poses a threat due to its voracious appetite and inedible nature. To combat this issue, the European Union has implemented compensation schemes, paying fishermen to catch toadfish, which are then incinerated. Additionally, a project involving scuba divers culls lionfish in protected areas. A new initiative aims to turn this problem into an opportunity by promoting lionfish as a tavern delicacy. Campaigns like #TasteTheOcean encourage chefs and influencers to feature lionfish, and local restaurants are beginning to add it to their menus. Despite initial unfamiliarity, customers are finding lionfish to be a tasty and competitively priced alternative to traditional fish. This effort seeks to both manage the environmental threat and support the struggling fishing industry.
