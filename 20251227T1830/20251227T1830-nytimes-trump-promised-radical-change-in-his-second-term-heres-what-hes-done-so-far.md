## Article summary
One year into his second term, Donald J. Trump has enacted or sought fundamental changes across policy, politics, and society, driving illegal border crossings to record lows and limiting non-white refugee admissions. He has eliminated diversity programs in government and pushed corporations to do the same. Trump has also played a role in a Gaza cease-fire, threatened aid to Ukraine, and deployed federal troops into U.S. cities. His administration has strained relations with allies and pursued policies that enriched his family and aides. Trump has upended the global trading system with import taxes and extended tax cuts from his first term, while reversing climate policies and slashing the federal workforce. He has governed extensively through emergency powers, leading to a polarizing first year. On immigration, he escalated crackdowns, blocking asylum access and pressuring Mexico, though falling short of mass deportation goals. The federal workforce has been significantly downsized, with agencies like USAID and the Education Department gutted. Trade policies have led to uncertainty and price increases, with few signs of a manufacturing boom. Affordability remains a concern, with persistent inflation despite some tariff relaxations. In combating illegal drugs, Trump has authorized aggressive military action against suspected drug smugglers at sea, a policy questioned by legal specialists. While he played a role in a Gaza cease-fire, his pledge to end the Ukraine war quickly failed. Trump has also normalized the use of federal troops for domestic law enforcement and openly used the Justice Department against perceived political enemies. His administration has purged diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives across the government and targeted private corporations embracing such practices. A core theme has been the accumulation of presidential power, with attempts to centralize control over the executive branch and assert authority over Congress's power of the purse.
