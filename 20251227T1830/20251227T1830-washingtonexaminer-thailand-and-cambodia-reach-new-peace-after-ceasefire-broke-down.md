## Article summary
Thailand and Cambodia have reached a new peace agreement following the breakdown of a previous ceasefire brokered by President Donald Trump. The initial ceasefire, signed in Malaysia, collapsed earlier this month due to mutual accusations of violations, leading to renewed border fighting. The new agreement, effective Saturday, calls for an immediate ceasefire monitored for 72 hours. If successful, Thailand will release 18 Cambodian soldiers held captive since July. The pact also includes provisions to halt the use of landmines and the dissemination of fake news along their shared border, which had fueled tensions. Thai Defense Minister Nattaphon Narkphanit emphasized the monitoring period to confirm a continuous ceasefire. Cambodian Defense Minister Tea Seiha expressed hope that the stabilization would allow displaced civilians to return home, resume work, and send their children back to school. The recent conflict resulted in dozens of civilian deaths and injuries on both sides. A Thai official described the new agreement as a test of Cambodia's sincerity. Trump, who previously leveraged threats of trade restrictions to facilitate a peace deal, was absent from the latest talks. Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, whose country brokered the initial ceasefire, voiced optimism for the new accord, highlighting the need for restraint in the interest of civilians.
